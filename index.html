Setup guide

Connecting your own payment, email and sign-in accounts

Hosted Online Live documentation site: https://josemodi97.github.io/reactive-demo-docs/

Reactive Shop talks to PayPal, M-Pesa, Pesapal, an email server and Google. Every one of these must be your own account, so the money, the emails and the customer data are yours. This guide walks through each provider, from a free test (sandbox) account to taking real payments, with screenshots of every screen.

PayPal

Cards and PayPal balance. Free sandbox; Business account to go live.

M-Pesa Express

STK push on the shopper's phone. Free sandbox; a paybill or till to go live.

Pesapal

Mobile money and cards across East Africa. Public sandbox keys; merchant account to go live.

Email (SMTP)

Order confirmations and password resets. Brevo, Gmail or your web host.

Google sign-in

Optional "Continue with Google". Free Firebase project.
You only need what you'll use. Each service is optional and switched off until it's set up: checkout only offers the payment methods you've configured, and the shop works without email or Google sign-in. Payments are tested in sandbox (fake money) before you switch them to production.
1

Before you start

Where the settings go, sandbox vs production, and the one address everything needs.

Where you enter the keys: Admin → Integrations

Sign in as the admin and open Integrations in the sidebar. Every service has a card with its fields, a Sandbox / Production switch, separate settings for each environment, a Test button and an Offered at checkout switch. Changes apply straight away, without restarting the shop.

Your keys stay private. Secrets you save here (consumer secrets, client secrets, SMTP passwords) are stored encrypted in integration-settings.json next to the app. The encryption key is shop.secrets-key in shop-install.properties (or the SHOP_SECRETS_KEY environment variable). Back that file up and keep it private: without it, the saved secrets can't be read.

Sandbox first, production later

Each payment provider has two separate sets of keys:

  • Sandbox: a test copy of the provider. No real money moves; you pay with test accounts and test phone numbers.
  • Production (also called live): real payments into your account. Usually needs a verified business account.

In each card, fill in Edit settings for: Sandbox, test, and later fill in the Production tab and flip Environment in use to Production. Payments already started finish in the environment they began in.

Your shop's public address

Payment providers send the shopper back to your shop, and post payment results to it. They need an address reachable from the internet, over HTTPS in production. Set it once and the callback URLs in Integrations follow it:

SHOP_PUBLIC_URL=https://shop.example.com
Testing on your own computer? http://localhost:8080 can't receive M-Pesa or Pesapal notifications, because their servers can't reach your computer. Either test those two on a server, or run a tunnel such as Cloudflare Tunnel or ngrok and use its https address as SHOP_PUBLIC_URL.
2

PayPal

PayPal balance and cards, with PayPal's buttons inside the checkout dialog. Sandbox: freeLive: PayPal Business account

Create your sandbox app

  1. Sign in at developer.paypal.com with your PayPal account (or create one). Make sure the switch at the top left says Sandbox, then open Apps & Credentials.
  2. Click Create App. Give it a name (e.g. your shop's name), keep the type Merchant, leave the suggested Sandbox Business Account, and click Create App.
  3. The app opens on its API credentials. Copy the Client ID, then click the eye or copy icon next to Secret key 1 and copy the secret.
  4. In the shop, open Admin → Integrations → PayPal, stay on Edit settings for: Sandbox, paste the Client ID and Client secret, and click Save sandbox, then Test. Switch Offered at checkout on.
Currencies. PayPal doesn't accept Kenyan shillings (KES). The Currencies field lists what PayPal may charge in (default USD,EUR,GBP,CAD,AUD); shoppers using another currency are charged in your shop currency if it's listed, otherwise in the first one.

Test a payment

PayPal creates two test accounts for you under Testing Tools → Sandbox Accounts: a Business one that receives the money and a Personal one to pay with. Open the Personal account's ⋮ → View/edit account to see its email and password, then check out in your shop with PayPal and log in to the PayPal window with it.

Going live

  1. Upgrade to (or sign up for) a PayPal Business account and confirm your email and bank details with PayPal.
  2. On developer.paypal.com switch the toggle to Live, open Apps & Credentials, click Create App, and copy the live Client ID and Secret.
  3. In Integrations → PayPal, open Edit settings for: Production, paste them, save, test, then set Environment in use to Production.
3

M-Pesa Express (Safaricom Daraja)

The shopper gets an M-Pesa PIN prompt on their phone (STK push). Sandbox: freeLive: an M-Pesa paybill or till

Create your sandbox app

  1. Sign up or log in at developer.safaricom.co.ke and open My Apps from your account menu. Click Create Sandbox App.
  2. Name the app (letters, numbers, spaces and underscores only), tick Lipa Na M-Pesa Sandbox (the product behind M-Pesa Express), and click Create App.
  3. Your app appears in My Apps with M-PESA EXPRESS Sandbox. Use the copy icons next to Consumer Key and Consumer Secret.
  4. The sandbox Business ShortCode (174379) and Passkey are on the Test Credentials page; they're the same for every developer. The shop already fills them in for sandbox.
  5. In the shop, open Integrations → M-Pesa Express, paste the Consumer key and Consumer secret, check the Callback URL is your public https address followed by /api/payment/callback, then Save sandbox, Test, and switch Offered at checkout on.
The callback must be public HTTPS. Safaricom posts the payment result to the callback URL. On localhost the prompt still reaches the phone, but your shop never hears the result. See Before you start for testing with a tunnel.

Going live

  1. You need an M-Pesa paybill or till number with Lipa Na M-Pesa Online enabled, and the username of its Business Administrator or Business Manager on the M-Pesa Organization portal.
  2. On Daraja open Go Live, enter your organisation's shortcode, name and M-Pesa username, accept the terms, and confirm with the one-time code sent to that user's phone.
  3. A production app appears under My Apps → Production Apps with its own consumer key and secret. Safaricom gives you the production passkey for your shortcode as part of going live.
  4. In Integrations → M-Pesa Express, open Edit settings for: Production, enter the production key, secret, your shortcode and passkey, save, test, then set Environment in use to Production.
4

Pesapal

Mobile money and cards on Pesapal's hosted payment page. Sandbox: public demo keysLive: Pesapal merchant account

Sandbox

Pesapal publishes ready-made demo merchant keys for each country, so there's no sandbox sign-up:

  1. Open developer.pesapal.com/api3-demo-keys.txt and copy the consumer_key and consumer_secret for your country (e.g. Kenyan Merchant).
  2. In Integrations → Pesapal, keep the API base URL at the sandbox address https://cybqa.pesapal.com/pesapalv3, paste the key and secret, save, test, and switch Offered at checkout on.

Going live

  1. Open a merchant account at pesapal.com and complete Pesapal's business verification.
  2. Get your live consumer key and secret from Pesapal for that merchant account (from your merchant dashboard, or ask Pesapal support if they aren't shown there).
  3. In Integrations → Pesapal → Production, set the API base URL to https://pay.pesapal.com/v3, paste the live key and secret, save, test, and set Environment in use to Production.
The IPN URL must be reachable from the internet. Pesapal confirms each payment by calling /api/payments/pesapal/ipn on your shop. The shop registers this address with Pesapal by itself; it only has to be a public https address.

Email (SMTP)

Order confirmations, password resets, email verification and copies of notifications.

The shop sends email through any SMTP server. Pick one:

Brevo Recommended

A transactional email service.

  • Free plan: 300 emails a day
  • Best delivery to inboxes
  • Needs a domain you own for the sender address

Gmail

Your Google account.

  • Quick to try
  • Sending limits (about 500 a day)
  • Mail comes from your Gmail address

Your web host

The mailbox that comes with your hosting (cPanel, Plesk…).

  • Already paid for
  • Mail from your own domain
  • Delivery depends on the host

Option A: Brevo

  1. Create a free account at brevo.com. Open Settings → SMTP & API. The SMTP tab shows the server, port and your login.
  2. Click Generate SMTP key. The first time, Brevo asks you to verify a phone number.
  3. Name the key (e.g. your shop's name) and click Generate. Copy it immediately: Brevo shows it only once. It then appears in the list, masked.
  4. Under Senders, domains, IPs, add the address your shop sends from and authenticate its domain (Brevo shows the DNS records to add at your domain registrar). A free-mail sender such as a Gmail address is flagged, and its mail is likely to land in spam.
Shop fieldBrevo value
SMTP hostsmtp-relay.brevo.com
Port / Security587 / STARTTLS
UsernameThe Login on Brevo's SMTP page (…@smtp-brevo.com), not your own email
PasswordThe SMTP key
From addressA sender on your authenticated domain, e.g. orders@yourshop.com

Option B: Gmail (app password)

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification for the Google account at myaccount.google.com/security. App passwords need it.
  2. Open myaccount.google.com/apppasswords, type a name such as Reactive Shop, and click Create.
  3. Copy the 16-letter password Google shows (spaces don't matter). It's shown only once.
Shop fieldGmail value
SMTP hostsmtp.gmail.com
Port / Security587 / STARTTLS (or 465 / SSL)
UsernameYour full Gmail address
PasswordThe 16-letter app password (not your Google password)
From addressThe same Gmail address (Gmail rewrites any other)
Fine for a small shop or trying things out. Consumer Gmail accounts can send to about 500 recipients a day, and mail always comes from your Gmail address. For a growing shop, use Brevo or your domain's mailbox.

Option C: your web host's mailbox (cPanel and similar)

  1. In your hosting panel, create a mailbox such as orders@yourshop.com (cPanel: Email Accounts → Create).
  2. Find its settings (cPanel: Email Accounts → Connect Devices → Mail Client Manual Settings). Use the outgoing server values.
Shop fieldTypical value
SMTP hostmail.yourshop.com (or the server name the panel shows)
Port / Security465 / SSL, or 587 / STARTTLS

PasswordThe mailbox password
From addressThe same mailbox address

Enter it in the shop

Open Integrations → Email (SMTP), fill in the fields from your option's table, Save, then click Test: it sends a test email to the From address. Switch Send emails on.

Google sign-in (Firebase)

Optional "Continue with Google" button and One Tap prompt. Email and password sign-in works without it. Free

You need four values from a Firebase project you own. All four are public by design (they're sent to browsers); no private key or service-account file is needed.

  1. Create a project. At console.firebase.google.com click Create a new Firebase project, name it, and note the project ID shown under the name. Gemini and Google Analytics can be switched off; the shop doesn't use them. Firebase requires 2-Step Verification on your Google account.
  2. Turn on Google sign-in. Authentication → Get started → Sign-in method → Google, switch Enable on, set the public-facing name (your shop's name) and support email, and Save. Open the Google row again → Web SDK configuration and copy the Web client ID (ends in .apps.googleusercontent.com).
  3. Register a web app. Project settings (gear) → General → Your apps → Web (</>), give it a nickname and Register app. From the config it shows, copy apiKey, authDomain and projectId; ignore the rest.
  4. Allow your domain. Authentication → Settings → Authorized domains → Add domain: your shop's domain (localhost is already there). For One Tap, also add https://yourshop.com under Authorized JavaScript origins of the Web client (auto created by Google Service) in Google Cloud → Google Auth Platform → Clients. It can take a few minutes to a few hours to apply.
  5. Enter them in the shop: Integrations → Google sign-in, then Test and Save.
Shop fieldFrom Firebase
Firebase project IDprojectId (Project settings → General)
Web API keyapiKey
Auth domainauthDomain, usually <project-id>.firebaseapp.com
OAuth web client IDThe Web client ID from Authentication → Sign-in method → Google

Transfer to Production

Complete walkthrough for moving from sandbox testing to a live, secure public store.

Zero-Downtime Environment Switcher: Reactive Shop maintains separate Sandbox and Production credentials for every gateway inside integration-settings.json. You can configure and verify live keys in the background while running tests, and switch to live with a single click in Admin → Integrations without restarting the application. In-flight sandbox checkouts finish safely in sandbox, while all subsequent orders immediately use production gateways.

1. Server, domain and HTTPS reverse proxy

Payment gateways (especially M-Pesa Express and Pesapal) require a public, trusted HTTPS callback endpoint. They will refuse connections to localhost or unencrypted HTTP.

  1. Assign a domain and public IP: Point your domain's DNS A/AAAA records (e.g. shop.example.com) to your production server.
  2. Set your public URL: Set SHOP_PUBLIC_URL=https://shop.example.com (no trailing slash). All payment redirect URLs, callbacks, and order emails follow this setting.
  3. Run behind a reverse proxy (Nginx or Caddy) with SSL: Obtain a free SSL certificate with Let's Encrypt (Certbot) or Cloudflare SSL.
  4. Forward client IP headers: Set SHOP_FORWARD_HEADERS=framework so the built-in Netty rate limiters track real visitor IPs instead of grouping all traffic under your reverse proxy's IP.
# Sample Nginx reverse proxy configuration:
server {
    listen 80;
    server_name shop.example.com;
    return 301 https://$host$request_uri;
}

server {
    listen 443 ssl http2;
    server_name shop.example.com;

    ssl_certificate     /etc/letsencrypt/live/shop.example.com/fullchain.pem;
    ssl_certificate_key /etc/letsencrypt/live/shop.example.com/privkey.pem;

    location / {
        proxy_pass http://127.0.0.1:8080;
        proxy_set_header Host $host;
        proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-For $proxy_add_x_forwarded_for;
        proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-Proto $scheme;
        proxy_set_header X-Forwarded-Port $server_port;

        # Keep server-sent event (SSE) live streams open
        proxy_buffering off;
        proxy_read_timeout 3600s;
    }
}

2. Production database and backups

In-memory H2 is intended only for development and sandbox demos; all data disappears when the application restarts. Production requires a durable, persistent database engine.

  • Supported engines: MariaDB 10.6+, MySQL 8.0+, PostgreSQL 14+, Microsoft SQL Server 2019+, or Oracle 19c+.
  • Initial install: When launching on a clean database, the wizard at /setup.html automatically tests your connection, checks collation, and executes all Flyway migrations (tables, sequences, initial catalog, and settings).
  • Automated migrations: Future application updates automatically migrate the schema forward without manual SQL scripts.
  • Backups: Schedule regular automated backups of your database (e.g. daily cron with mysqldump or pg_dump).

3. Safety checks and secrets hardening

When started with SHOP_PRODUCTION=true (the default), the shop runs strict safety assertions (ProductionSafetyCheck) and will refuse to boot if dangerous development defaults are detected.

Security SettingProduction RequirementPurpose
JWT_SECRETUnique random string, at least 32 charactersSigns customer and admin authentication tokens. Replaces the known development key.
ADMIN_PASSWORDAt least 12 characters (not admin123)Protects initial administrative access.
SHOP_SECRETS_KEYMust be saved and backed up privatelyAES encryption key that secures stored gateway credentials in integration-settings.json. Automatically generated into shop-install.properties on first install.
SHOP_CORS_ALLOWED_ORIGINSEmpty, or strictly your own domainsBlocks unauthorized third-party browsers from making authenticated API calls. Never set to * or localhost in production.
Back up shop-install.properties: This file holds your installation's secrets key. Keep an encrypted copy off-server. Without this key, stored payment secrets cannot be decrypted after moving to a new server.

4. Switching each gateway to live

Open Admin → Integrations in your browser. For each service you plan to offer to real shoppers, perform the live configuration switch:

  1. PayPal
    • In developer.paypal.com, flip the top toggle to Live and open Apps & Credentials.
    • Create or select your live merchant app, and copy the live Client ID and Secret.
    • In shop Admin → Integrations → PayPal, select Edit settings for: Production.
    • Paste your live Client ID and Secret. Ensure your accepted currencies are configured.
    • Click Save production, then click Test.
    • Set Environment in use to Production and flip Offered at checkout to ON.
    • Tip: In your PayPal Business account settings (Website Payments), ensure PayPal Account Optional is enabled so shoppers can pay directly with debit/credit cards without creating a PayPal account.
  2. M-Pesa Express (Safaricom Daraja)
    • Log in to developer.safaricom.co.ke and open Go Live. Submit your live Paybill or Till Shortcode and authorized Business Administrator details.
    • Under My Apps → Production Apps, copy your production Consumer Key and Consumer Secret. Safaricom provides your production Passkey upon approval.
    • In shop Admin → Integrations → M-Pesa, switch to Edit settings for: Production.
    • Enter your production Consumer Key, Consumer Secret, Business Shortcode, and Passkey.
    • Verify the Callback URL is https://shop.example.com/api/payment/callback.
    • Click Save production, test, and flip Environment in use to Production.
  3. Pesapal
    • Log in to your verified business account at pesapal.com.
    • Retrieve your live Consumer Key and Consumer Secret from the merchant dashboard.
    • In shop Admin → Integrations → Pesapal, select Edit settings for: Production.
    • Change the API base URL to the live endpoint: https://pay.pesapal.com/v3.
    • Paste the live credentials. Verify IPN URL is https://shop.example.com/api/payments/pesapal/ipn.
    • Click Save production, test, and flip Environment in use to Production.
  4. Transactional email (SMTP)
    • Use a dedicated transactional provider (Brevo, Amazon SES, SendGrid, or your web host).
    • Ensure your domain DNS records include valid SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records matching your provider.
    • In shop Admin → Integrations → Email, enter the production SMTP server, port, credentials, and a verified From address (e.g. orders@yourdomain.com).
    • Click Test and confirm receipt in your personal inbox (check that it does not land in spam). Flip Send emails to ON.
  5. Google sign-in (Firebase)
    • In Firebase Console → Authentication → Settings → Authorized domains, add shop.example.com.
    • In Google Cloud Console → APIs & Services → Credentials → OAuth 2.0 Web Client, add https://shop.example.com under Authorized JavaScript origins for One Tap sign-in.

5. Clearing test data: Restart store fresh

During sandbox testing, test orders, fake transactions, and test carts were created, temporarily reducing inventory. Before welcoming real customers, purge this testing history:

  1. Log in as admin and navigate to Admin → Overview.
  2. Click Restart store fresh.
  3. Confirm the prompt.
What gets cleaned: All test orders and line items are purged; reserved catalog stock is automatically restored to full original inventory; all guest and customer carts are cleared; payment attempt logs are reset; and invoice/order sequence numbers restart clean. Your catalog products, categories, pricing, branding, and integration credentials remain completely intact.

6. Live smoke-testing and refund

Never declare a shop ready without verifying a real end-to-end payment with real currency:

  1. Temporarily create a private test product priced at the minimum allowable amount (e.g. $1.00 USD or 10 KES), or add a low-cost item to cart.
  2. Complete checkout as a customer using your real card or mobile phone.
  3. Verify that the gateway immediately authorizes the charge, redirects back to your order confirmation, and transitions the order status from PENDING to PAID.
  4. Confirm that an automated order confirmation email arrives in your inbox.
  5. Sign into your merchant dashboard (PayPal, Pesapal, or M-Pesa portal) and issue a full refund for the test order. Confirm the refund status in the provider console.

7. Running as a continuous service (Systemd)

On Linux servers (Ubuntu, Debian, RHEL), configure Systemd so the application starts on boot and restarts automatically if terminated:

# /etc/systemd/system/reactive-shop.service
[Unit]
Description=Reactive Shop E-Commerce Engine
After=network.target

[Service]
Type=simple
User=shop
WorkingDirectory=/var/www/reactive-shop
ExecStart=/usr/bin/java -Xms512m -Xmx2g -jar reactive-shop.jar
Restart=always
RestartSec=10
EnvironmentFile=/var/www/reactive-shop/.env

# Security sandbox
NoNewPrivileges=true
PrivateTmp=true

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target

Reload and enable: sudo systemctl daemon-reload && sudo systemctl enable --now reactive-shop.

8. Pre-launch go-live checklist

  • Public domain configured with valid SSL/HTTPS certificate.
  • SHOP_PUBLIC_URL=https://yourdomain.com configured and verified.
  • Production database (MariaDB / MySQL / PostgreSQL / SQL Server / Oracle) active with automated backups.
  • JWT_SECRET set to a strong 32+ character random secret.
  • Admin password is strong (12+ characters); initial test admin passwords rotated.
  • shop-install.properties safely backed up off-server.
  • Each offered gateway tested in production mode and flipped to Production in Integrations.
  • Transactional email verified with DKIM/SPF DNS records and test email delivered to inbox.
  • Production domain added to Firebase Authorized Domains and OAuth JavaScript origins.
  • Test orders and carts cleared via Admin → Overview → Restart store fresh.
  • Live real-money smoke test verified and refunded.
  • Systemd service or Docker daemon configured with auto-restart on boot.

Troubleshooting

What you seeLikely cause and fix
A payment method doesn't appear at checkoutIts card isn't complete, or Offered at checkout is off. The card lists what's missing.
PayPal: "Client Authentication failed"Sandbox keys used with Production (or the reverse), or a copy-paste slip. Check which tab you edited and which environment is in use.
M-Pesa prompt arrives but the order stays pendingThe callback URL isn't public HTTPS, so Safaricom can't deliver the result. Fix SHOP_PUBLIC_URL or use a tunnel.
M-Pesa: "Invalid Access Token" / 401Consumer key or secret wrong, or from an app without the Lipa Na M-Pesa product.
Pesapal payments stay pendingPesapal can't reach the IPN URL. It must be your public https address.
Email test fails with "Authentication failed"Brevo: the username must be the …@smtp-brevo.com login. Gmail: use an app password, not your Google password.
Emails land in spamThe From address's domain isn't authenticated (DKIM / DMARC). Use your own domain and add the records your provider gives you.
Google: auth/unauthorized-domainAdd your domain under Firebase Authentication → Settings → Authorized domains.
One Tap: "origin is not allowed"Add your https origin to the Google client's Authorized JavaScript origins, then wait for it to apply.
